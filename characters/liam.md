# Liam

- **Role:** rebellious teenager
- **Wants:** to find his place in the world
- **Obstacle:** pushes against boundaries that seem to constrict him at
  every turn
- **Backstory:** Liam Marsh is fifteen and has lived in Willow Creek his
  whole life. He doesn't remember his father, who left when Liam was two
  and calls on birthdays about half the time. He picked up guitar two years
  ago on a cracked-body pawnshop instrument he's never replaced, and writes
  lyrics obsessively in a spiral notebook held together with tape, mostly
  about leaving. He's gotten into minor trouble — skipping class, a bit of
  trespassing, hanging around older kids his mother doesn't love — less out
  of real defiance than because it's the only reliable way he's found to
  get a reaction out of a house where his mother is too tired most nights
  to give him one. Poking around the library's newspaper archive this fall
  — half a school assignment, half his own restlessness — put Daniel Voss's
  disappearance in front of him for the first time, and he's latched onto
  it the way he latches onto everything lately: obsessively, publicly,
  with no sense yet of who it might actually belong to.
- **Voice / persona:** Present-tense-feeling even in past-tense narration —
  sensory, immediate, impatient with its own scene-setting. Sentences run
  on when he's excited and chop into fragments when he's not, reflecting a
  restlessness that hasn't found a productive outlet yet. What he notices
  first in a space: sound and social hierarchy — who's watching, who's
  performing, what's playing — before the physical space itself. Diction is
  contemporary and informal, and slides into lyric fragments or private
  commentary when he's turning something into a line for the notebook,
  which he does more than he'd admit to anyone. Metaphor source: music —
  rhythm, hooks, noise versus signal, a feeling that "won't scan." Voice
  test: "The room went quiet, the bad kind, the kind that's a held note
  about to break, and nobody else seemed to hear it but him."
- **Physical description:** An undercut he cut himself with kitchen
  scissors that came out uneven on one side, which he refuses to fix on
  principle. Callused fingertips on his left hand from a guitar he's never
  gotten properly set up — he's secretly proud of the calluses.
- **Internal want vs. external want:** Externally, he wants to matter to
  somebody — to make something (a song, a discovery, anything) that makes
  people look at him differently. Internally, he needs to be trusted with
  the truth by his mother rather than managed around it, which is the
  actual thing he's chasing every time he pushes a boundary.
- **Flaw:** Treats real consequences like a game or a project — digging for
  the thrill of finding something without registering what it might cost
  the people around him until it's already loose. It's exactly the
  mechanism by which his curiosity is going to surface his mother's secret.
- **Introduced:** Chapter 1, in his room, headphones on, writing lyrics
- **Arc** (chapter numbers per `manuscript/outline.md`'s 26-chapter table):
  - Ch. 2 — introduced, isolated in his room
  - Ch. 10 — the archive digitizing work and his mother's old photo box
    turn up something about Daniel ("The Notebook")
  - Ch. 20 — posts the connection publicly without understanding it
    ("Liam's Reckoning") — forces everyone's hand faster than they wanted
  - Ch. 24 — his digging puts him in visible risk at the climax, which is
    what finally makes Olivia speak
  - Ch. 25 — resolution with Olivia
- **Relationships:** Olivia's son (confirmed).
- **Plot thread (resolved 2026-09-14):** doesn't know the town's history
  around Daniel's disappearance. His digging around — curiosity, not
  malice — is what eventually surfaces the connection between Michael,
  Emma's family, and Olivia's silence, going public around Ch. 20 ("Liam's
  Reckoning").
- **Key relationships (beyond Olivia):**
  - **Sarah:** Has seen her paintings of the lake in the café window
    downtown and thinks the recurring image is "kind of metal" without
    knowing why it gets under his skin — one of the loose threads feeding
    his curiosity about the old case.
  - **Michael:** Clocks him early as someone who doesn't belong in town
    and, half as a game, starts tracking where he goes and what he's
    asking about — completely unaware they're chasing the same
    eighteen-year-old night.
  - **Jack:** Gets caught by him digging through the library's old
    newspaper archive; instead of the lecture he expects, gets something
    closer to recognition, and an unlikely, half-unspoken mentorship starts
    there.
  - **Emma:** Has been caught loitering near Hale Development's side
    entrance by the records archive twice, which everyone including Emma
    has chalked up to petty teenage mischief.
