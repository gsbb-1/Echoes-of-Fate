# Michael

- **Role:** mysterious stranger, newcomer to Willow Creek
- **Wants:** searching for something he can't yet put into words
- **Obstacle:** a troubled past he keeps hidden behind a stoic facade
- **Backstory:** Michael Voss was nineteen when his older brother Daniel,
  twenty-two, took a seasonal harvest job at the Hale family's orchard and
  never called home again. The Voss family lived an hour away, in the next
  county. In family lore that nobody ever quite verified, Daniel had also
  started asking questions about an old boundary dispute tied to their
  grandfather's land — a story that calcified after he vanished, retold by
  relatives until nobody remembers whether Daniel said it himself or
  whether it got added later to give his disappearance a reason. Michael's
  last conversation with Daniel was a fight over something that embarrasses
  him now with its smallness — a borrowed car, a missed birthday. He told
  Daniel he was being selfish and hung up. Their father spent a decade
  insisting Daniel had simply run off and died a few years ago still
  officially believing it. Their mother is alive, and asks Michael, every
  visit, if there's anything new; there rarely is. Michael has worked a
  string of short-haul jobs since, never more than a few months in one
  place, spending eighteen years not-quite-looking and not-quite-forgetting.
  Coming to Willow Creek now is the first time he's let himself actually
  look. *(Resolved by plot-research, 2026-09-14 — see
  `scenes/plot-map.md`: the "boundary dispute" is family myth with a
  kernel of truth. What Daniel actually found, late in the season, was an
  unfiled safety inspection flagging the old quarry pond's embankment as
  unstable — paperwork tied to the land, which eighteen years of retelling
  blurred into "a dispute over the grandfather's land." He confronted site
  foreman Hank Colby about it at the pond the night he died.)*
- **Voice / persona:** Short, withheld sentences built for observation, not
  reflection; narration tracks what a room contains before what it means,
  and resists naming his own feelings even in free indirect style — he'll
  notice his hand is shaking before he'll admit to himself he's afraid.
  What he notices first in a space: exits, hands, and small tells on other
  people (a held glance, a too-quick answer) before faces or names. Diction
  is plain, a little clipped, unsentimental almost defensively so — he
  interrupts his own thoughts before they get soft. Metaphor source: roads
  and transience — distances, mile markers, the specific tiredness of
  somewhere he hasn't unpacked yet — never anything rooted, because he
  hasn't let himself be. Voice test: "Two ways out of the diner, and the
  woman behind the counter had already clocked him twice."
- **Physical description:** Wears a cheap digital watch with a cracked
  face, frozen at 4:47 — it was Daniel's, found in his room after, and
  Michael has never replaced the battery. A thin white line through his
  left eyebrow from a bar fight he doesn't especially want to explain.
- **Internal want vs. external want:** Externally, he wants hard facts
  about what happened to Daniel — a timeline, a name, something he can put
  in front of his mother. Internally, he needs to forgive himself for the
  last thing he said to his brother, and to let himself stop treating every
  town he passes through as temporary.
- **Flaw:** Treats information as a substitute for connection — questions
  people to extract what he needs, then withdraws before anything can be
  asked of him in return. It's exactly the instinct that will damage things
  with Sarah once she notices she's been, in part, a source.
- **Introduced:** Chapter 2, walking Main Street, cataloguing exits and the
  postcard in his jacket pocket
- **Arc** (chapter numbers per `manuscript/outline.md`'s 26-chapter table):
  - Ch. 2 — introduced, new in town, searching for something
  - Ch. 4 — meets Sarah properly
  - Ch. 7 — brings Daniel's postcard to Jack, informally reopening the case
  - Ch. 15 — relationship with Sarah deepens; his real reason for being in
    town starts to leak through
  - Ch. 21 — Alliances, as the Harvest Festival approaches
  - Ch. 23-24 — the climax; he's the one who connects the watch in Sarah's
    painting to the one recovered with Daniel's remains
  - Ch. 26 — resolution with Sarah, past the mystery that brought them
    together
- **Relationships:** Sarah (love interest / catalyst)
- **Plot thread (resolved 2026-09-14):** Michael's older brother, Daniel,
  disappeared from Willow Creek eighteen years ago and was never found —
  Jack's unsolved case. Michael has come to town to find out what actually
  happened, working from what little he knows of Daniel's last days there.
- **Key relationships (beyond Sarah):**
  - **Jack:** Michael's first instinct is that Jack is the detective who
    failed to solve his brother's case, and he approaches him coldly,
    almost accusatory, before grudgingly recognizing how much of the file
    Jack kept out of duty rather than obligation.
  - **Emma:** Michael goes looking for old property and boundary records
    tied to the rumored Voss land dispute, which puts him in Hale
    Development's office asking Emma questions she has to work hard to
    answer without lying outright.
  - **Olivia:** They've barely spoken, but Michael carries the same
    unplaceable, prickling wariness around her that a lot of people in town
    seem to have without knowing why — and she has a sharper version of the
    same reaction to him that she hasn't let herself examine.
  - **Liam:** Liam clocks Michael almost immediately as someone who doesn't
    belong and starts, half as a game, tracking where he goes — which is
    how Liam's digging and Michael's search end up running parallel long
    before they formally meet.
