# Jack

- **Role:** retired detective
- **Wants:** closure on the case that never got solved
- **Obstacle:** the unsolved case still haunts him; retirement hasn't given
  him peace
- **Backstory:** Jack Muldoon spent thirty-one years on Willow Creek's small
  police force, the last eleven as its only detective, which in a town this
  size meant handling everything from break-ins to the one case that never
  closed. He led the search for Daniel Voss eighteen years ago — three
  weeks of volunteer search lines through the orchard and the woods above
  the lake, divers in the water twice, nothing. He interviewed a teenage
  Olivia Marsh three times and always had the sense, never provable, that
  she was holding something back; it's the interview he still replays. He
  tore his ACL falling into the ravine above the lake during the second
  search week, and it aches before rain to this day. He retired six years
  ago, not entirely by choice — the department pushed early retirement on
  him after his wife, Carol, told the chief privately that Jack hadn't
  fully come home from that case even a decade later. Carol left two years
  after that; they're on decent terms now, mostly by staying out of each
  other's way. He still keeps his own copy of the Voss file, unofficially,
  in a box in the closet, and updates it out of habit more than hope.
  *(Resolved by plot-research, 2026-09-14 — see `scenes/plot-map.md`: the
  case reopens in two stages. First, informally — Michael arrives with a
  postcard Daniel mailed their mother days before he vanished, mentioning
  he'd found something Hale "wasn't going to like," and asks Jack to look
  at it. Later, officially — Hale Development finally breaks ground on the
  long-stalled lakeside parcel and grading equipment turns up remains.)*
- **Voice / persona:** Procedural rhythm even at rest — clauses that
  catalog time, place, who was where, a habit thirty years of reports left
  in him permanently. What he notices first in a space: what's out of
  place — a car parked somewhere it shouldn't be, a door that should be
  locked and isn't — before he notices anything about the people
  themselves. Diction is dry, understated, allergic to sentimentality even
  about his own grief, which reads as restraint rather than coldness once
  you're close enough to see the restraint costing him something. Metaphor
  source: casework — timelines, witness statements, chain of custody; a
  feeling he'll clock as "circumstantial" before he'll call it a feeling.
  Voice test: "Wrong time of day for that car to be parked there, and he'd
  stopped believing in coincidence somewhere around year two."
- **Physical description:** Rope-callused hands from search-and-rescue
  volunteer work he still does most weekends, more than the job requires. A
  knee that announces weather before the forecast does, from the ravine
  fall eighteen years ago.
- **Internal want vs. external want:** Externally, he wants an answer to
  Daniel's case he can put down on paper and close, literally, in the file.
  Internally, he needs absolution for what the case cost the people close
  to him — Carol, his own kids, functionally raised by a man who was always
  half-listening — which finding Daniel won't actually give him, though
  he's built his life around believing it will.
- **Flaw:** Interviews people even when he isn't working a case, including
  the people who love him — treating conversation as information-gathering
  rather than connection. It cost him his marriage once already, and it's
  exactly what risks happening again if he pushes Michael or Olivia before
  either is ready.
- **Introduced:** Chapter 1, sitting on a park bench, watching children play
- **Arc** (chapter numbers per `manuscript/outline.md`'s 26-chapter table):
  - Ch. 1 — introduced, still haunted by the old case
  - Ch. 7 — Michael brings him Daniel's postcard; the case reopens
    informally
  - Ch. 8 — reopens his old case notes; the new lead starts to rhyme with it
  - Ch. 13 — starts asking around; unsettles people who thought this was
    long closed
  - Ch. 18 — has to decide how far to push Emma
  - Ch. 25-26 — finally closes the file; his own reckoning
- **Relationships:** connected to Emma via the new case (see `emma.md`)
- **Plot thread (resolved 2026-09-14):** the unsolved case is Daniel's
  disappearance eighteen years ago (see `michael.md`). It reopens via
  Michael's postcard, then officially via the remains found at the
  Hale groundbreaking — full sequence in `scenes/plot-map.md`.
- **Key relationships (beyond Emma):**
  - **Sarah:** He's known her since she was small enough to be underfoot in
    her parents' shop and still calls her "kiddo." He was on the ground the
    week her family's old lake cabin came up peripherally in his case
    notes, though he's never said so to her and isn't sure it's relevant —
    its own kind of lie of omission, for a man who prides himself on not
    telling them.
  - **Michael:** His first read on Michael is textbook — a relative running
    his own parallel investigation, which he's seen go badly before — so
    he's cagier with him than he means to be, even as some part of him
    recognizes a man doing exactly what Jack himself never stopped doing.
  - **Olivia:** Their history is the most loaded of any two people in town
    who've barely spoken in years — he still remembers exactly which of her
    answers didn't sit right, and she still remembers exactly how carefully
    he looked at her.
  - **Liam:** Catches him digging through the library's old newspaper
    archive and, instead of running him off, recognizes the same
    compulsive curiosity he had at that age — an unlikely, half-unspoken
    mentorship starts there.
