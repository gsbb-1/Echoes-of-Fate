# Olivia

- **Role:** single mother, working multiple jobs
- **Wants:** to provide for her son and keep him from seeing the strain she's
  under
- **Obstacle:** constant juggling of bills and responsibilities
- **Backstory:** Olivia Marsh grew up in Willow Creek, the daughter of a
  construction worker who took seasonal crew jobs with Hale Development
  whenever the orchard or the outskirts apartments were being built out.
  She was seventeen the autumn Daniel Voss disappeared, working part-time
  at the diner and spending most of her free evenings down by the lake with
  an older crowd she wasn't really supposed to be running with. She was one
  of the last people to see Daniel, at the edge of the orchard, and
  whatever she saw or half-saw that night has never fully resolved itself
  even in her own memory — she's spent eighteen years telling herself she
  was seventeen and it was dark and she could be wrong, which is easier to
  live with than the alternative. Jack Muldoon interviewed her three times;
  she told him less each time, not more, once she understood how much
  weight her answers could carry. She still keeps a braided friendship
  bracelet from that summer in the bottom of a drawer she doesn't open.
  Liam's father left when Liam was two and isn't part of either of their
  lives; Olivia works the diner and cleans offices around town, including
  Hale Development's, two nights a week, to make up the difference.
  *(Resolved by plot-research, 2026-09-14 — see `scenes/plot-map.md`: she'd
  secretly arranged to meet Daniel at the orchard that night. Arriving
  late, she saw two figures struggling near the pond in poor light, heard
  raised voices, then a splash and silence — she didn't see the ground
  give way, only what looked like a fight. She glimpsed Warren Hale
  arriving by truck shortly after (responding to his foreman's panicked
  call) and has spent eighteen years believing she witnessed the
  aftermath of Hale killing Daniel — not the accident it actually was.)*
- **Voice / persona:** Economical, task-organized sentences — the rhythm of
  someone whose attention is permanently triaged and doesn't have the
  bandwidth for indulgent description ("Twenty minutes till the bus. Soup
  first, then the bills."). What she notices first: cost and time before
  anything else, and what Liam needs before what she needs — so automatic
  she'd have trouble naming it as a pattern if asked. Diction is plain and
  practical, no wasted words, even in her own head. Under real strain,
  sentences don't get longer or more emotional, they get shorter and more
  clipped, narrowing toward the next immediate task rather than the feeling
  underneath it. Metaphor source: money and time — what she has enough of
  and what she doesn't, hours accounted for like cash. Voice test: "Bus in
  eleven minutes, and the soup could wait or the conversation could, and
  she chose, the way she always chose, without deciding to."
- **Physical description:** A faint burn scar along the inside of her right
  forearm from the diner's fryer line, which she's stopped noticing. Keeps
  a braided friendship bracelet from when she was seventeen at the bottom
  of a drawer she hasn't opened in years.
- **Internal want vs. external want:** Externally, she wants the bills
  covered and Liam kept from seeing how close to the edge they run.
  Internally, she needs to put down eighteen years of silence without it
  costing her the son she built her whole adult life protecting.
- **Flaw:** Manages every kind of trouble the same way she managed what she
  saw at seventeen — by going quiet and absorbing it alone rather than
  asking for help. It kept her safe once and is now the exact habit
  standing between her and telling Liam anything true before he finds it
  out some worse way.
- **Introduced:** Chapter 1, making dinner on an empty stomach
- **Arc** (chapter numbers per `manuscript/outline.md`'s 26-chapter table):
  - Ch. 1 — introduced, struggling silently
  - Ch. 9 — grinding routine; friction with Liam, who's started asking
    questions about the town's past
  - Ch. 16 — pushed toward the choice she's avoided for eighteen years
  - Ch. 22 — trust tested; her path finally crosses Emma's directly
  - Ch. 24 — goes to Jack and confesses what she saw, at the climax
  - Ch. 25 — resolution with Liam; her own self-forgiveness
- **Relationships:** Liam's mother (confirmed).
- **Plot thread (resolved 2026-09-14):** as a teenager, Olivia was one of
  the last people to see Daniel (Michael's brother) before he disappeared
  eighteen years ago. She's never told anyone what she saw. That silence,
  not just financial strain, is the real weight she's carrying in Ch. 1.
  What finally makes her speak isn't the remains being found — it's fear
  for Liam, once his digging puts him at visible risk of getting too close
  to a history he doesn't understand (`scenes/plot-map.md`).
- **Key relationships (beyond Liam):**
  - **Jack:** The three interviews he ran her through at seventeen never
    fully left either of them; she still tenses at the sight of his truck
    in town, and he still can't quite let the case file's account of her
    testimony go.
  - **Sarah:** She babysat Sarah a handful of times as a young teenager and
    still half thinks of her as "the Bennetts' quiet kid," with no idea the
    two of them are circling the same eighteen-year-old night from opposite
    directions.
  - **Michael:** Something about him puts her on edge in a way she can't
    account for and doesn't examine too closely, on the general principle
    that examining things too closely has never gone well for her.
  - **Emma:** Cleans Hale Development's offices two nights a week; it has
    never once occurred to her that anyone else in town might also be
    keeping an eighteen-year-old secret.
