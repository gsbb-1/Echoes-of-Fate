# Scene plan: Chapter 11 — "Six Crossings"

Ensemble, three two-character scenes, one decision point per character.
Chapter thesis: the town feels smaller than usual — every thread brushing
the same place, the same decade, the same ground. No new facts break
surface; each character makes a first *active* decision this chapter.

- **Scene A — Michael & Emma (Hale Development office).** Michael asks for
  old survey/boundary records for the lakeside parcel, framed as a vague
  "family history" (the grandfather-land story is partly myth, per
  `characters/michael.md` and `scenes/plot-map.md`). Priya, whose antenna
  is already up from the Colby records gap she found at the groundbreaking
  (Ch. 6), flags the stranger. Emma gives a strictly-true polite denial —
  pre-digital records live at the county recorder's office; the company
  doesn't hold originals. Michael reads the real tell: she never once asked
  *why* he wanted them. Decisions:
  - **Michael:** files Emma under *knows something*; decides to go around
    her (county records, old crew) instead of pressing publicly — and, for
    the first time, decides the town is not a stopover: he stays until it
    answers.
  - **Emma:** the name "Voss" lands (the missing man's family, in her
    father's file). For the first time she stops running "not yet" and
    draws a real line under it: when the asking gets close, she will move
    the folder herself rather than let it be found. Withholding, but now a
    standing decision, not a daily deferral.
- **Scene B — Olivia & Sarah (diner), then Sarah at the parcel gate.** The
  festival is staffing up; Olivia takes the extra cleanup hours near the
  orchard-adjacent ground for the money, against her instinct. Sarah, at
  the counter, mentions she's been chasing "a place out past the lake" that
  keeps showing up in her work and asks if Olivia remembers the old land
  out there. Olivia deflects with a truth ("crew family — we went where the
  work was") and a dodge; the flinch registers on Sarah only as tiredness.
  Decisions:
  - **Olivia:** money over dread — she takes the festival work AND lets
    Liam keep the archive thread (won't be the one to make her son's
    restlessness about her old fear). Both are acts of doubled-down normal.
  - **Sarah (coda):** walks out to the parcel gate, sketches at the fence;
    the treeline/waterline resolves the "negative space" of her painting —
    the place is real, and some version of her has stood here before (age-6
    fragment, not consciously). She decides to come back with a canvas. NOT
    the family-sale/Hollis connection — that stays reserved for Ch. 17.
- **Scene C — Jack & Liam (archive).** Jack is at the reader again,
  working the reopened case; Liam is finishing his scan run. Their
  half-formed mentorship from Ch. 10 continues: Jack on "what's out of
  place," Liam's scan-itch about the search line. Jack notes, level,
  without pursuing: "Your mother grew up here." Liam: "She doesn't say
  anything." Jack lets the Olivia thread alone (his Ch. 8 discipline — the
  family the case touched is at his elbow). Decisions:
  - **Jack:** the informal reopen becomes an active inquiry — he'll walk
    the ground again, "before it stops being ground," and pull the old
    crew records (the Colby-retrieval stays reserved for Ch. 13).
  - **Liam:** makes it his own project — find the face at the edge of his
    mother's photograph, someone from that summer; keeps the two images
    private from her; doesn't post (posting is Ch. 20).
- **Crossings / irony plants to preserve:** Emma realizes Voss = the name
  in the folder's ground; Michael realizes Emma = the daughter of the
  postcard's "Hale"; Olivia realizes Sarah is circling the same
  eighteen-year-old night from the Bennetts' kid's direction; Jack realizes
  the son of the girl he interviewed is at his elbow; and Sarah (unknowing)
  tells people she's going to look at the very ground — while the reader
  knows what it holds.

## Craft notes
- Ensemble roving focus like Ch. 1-2, one focal register per scene; keep
  each scene under ~700 words; three scenes across `---` dividers.
- Decision beats should land as small actions (take a shift, walk a road,
  pull a record, set a canvas), not as declarations — show the cost in
  each voice (Emma's exact arithmetic, Olivia's triaged math, Jack's
  procedural commitment, Liam's run-on excitement).
