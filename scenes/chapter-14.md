# Scene plan: Chapter 14 — "What the File Says"

Emma POV. Jack's questions reach the company in person; she has to decide,
in real time, how much of the truth a controlled performance can hold.
She shows the transparent, safe layer (the parcel history binder Priya
built in Ch. 6 — whose one anomaly, the Colby records gap, is damning to
Colby but harmless to her) and holds back everything that points at her
father: the unfiled inspection, the note, the check, the red-ink ledger
entry. The chapter is the title's question: what the file says versus what
she says, and her growing awareness that Jack is walking the folder's
shape in order without ever holding it.

- **Beat 1 — Jack at Hale Development.** Priya knows him (retired
  detective). Jack asks, procedurally, for the parcel's 2008 site records
  — the crew, the foreman, anything on the north-bank regrade — and
  confirms (procedurally, not accusingly) that Daniel Voss worked that
  season. Emma receives him; stage-manages the binder; lets the Colby gap
  be seen (it looks like transparency; it feeds the herring only). The
  register of her deflection: true non-answers ("I wasn't running the
  company," "seasonal crews, my father ran a leaner office," "I don't
  have those originals") — the same she used with Priya (Ch. 6) and
  Michael (Ch. 11).
- **Beat 2 — the pressure.** Jack drops the postcard's line ("a man wrote
  his mother in the week before he died that he'd found something here
  Hale's not going to like") and, obliquely, that he's heard the old
  foreman came back to the valley. Emma's near-tell (Ch. 6's pattern): the
  three objects + the red-ink date surface unbidden; she catches her
  thumbnail against her back teeth and lets the machinery of herself run
  her out of it. She asks whether it's reopening officially; Jack: not yet,
  but "it won't stay that way if I keep finding the same shape."
- **Beat 3 — after Jack leaves: what the file says.** Alone, Emma re-reads
  the folder's three pieces with Jack's questions in mind (echo of Ch. 5)
  and plays his case against them: the folder still does not prove a
  death — only a cover-up — and that absence is the only thing still
  letting her sit on it. But Jack now holds the first three details in
  sequence without the folder, and the ledger (the one thing that would
  light the whole file) never came up. She makes Ch. 11's resolve
  specific: when it gets closer, she will be the one who moves it first —
  and "moving it first" now has a face (Jack's) and a cost (the company,
  her father's name, the families on the payroll). End on the watch / the
  locked drawer: the gap between what the file says and what she'll say is
  the whole of her secret life, and it is visibly starting to close.

## Craft notes
- Emma's controlled, exact voice; contract/land metaphors; over-precision
  under pressure. One genuine near-tell, pulled back the way Ch. 6 pulled
  it back. Priya is present only as a pressure valve (a reach toward the
  Colby file that Emma reroutes).
- No folder contents to Jack; no remains (those stay until Ch. 18); no
  Olivia. Do NOT have the ledger brought out — a near-miss line about it
  ("if he'd asked for the ledger") is the chapter's own internal gasp.
