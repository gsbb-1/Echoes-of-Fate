# Scene plan: Chapter 13 — "Digging Deeper"

Jack POV. Part III (Rising Action) opens with the informal reopen becoming
a real inquiry: asking around unsettles people who assumed the case was
long closed, and the Colby red herring gets planted (per
`scenes/plot-map.md` — his abrupt end-of-season departure reads, to Jack,
Michael, and the reader, as guilt of something worse than what actually
happened). The Colby *confrontation and admission* is deliberately deferred
to a later chapter (flag in TASK: schedule ~Ch. 19, before the climax).

- **Beat 1 — county records annex.** Jack pulls the 2008 season: Hale
  Development's crew registration (foreman: Hank Colby); the county issued
  NO grading permit for the parcel's north section that fall — confirming
  the regrade of the pond bank was unpermitted as well as undocumented
  (deepens Ch. 8's "no written work order"). No county record for Colby
  after 2008 — he has no local footprint since. Michael is using the same
  room (he's been here since Ch. 11's decision to go around Emma); they
  compare notes: Emma's polite denial + Jack's own read; Michael's "she
  never asked what I wanted it for." Both now hold the same shape: the
  foreman who was standing near the ground when Danny phoned home left
  town days after and vanished from every public record. Jack cautions
  Michael against raising it in town yet, knowing (a) it will unsettle
  people and (b) Jack himself is the one who can ask without starting a
  story.
- **Beat 2 — asking around / the town's guard.** The ordinary places Jack
  knows: the hardware bench, the gas station, the diner. The questions
  cost him what Ch. 8 predicted — Pete/Alcala-type friendliness closes
  ("I thought you were done, Muldoon"), Ruth's long look over the coffee
  pot, doors that stop a sentence short. The town line about Colby
  surfaces from an old-timer: the foreman cleared out overnight that
  autumn, didn't come back for his tools, "everybody said he knew
  something about the pond" — the herring, now public enough for Jack to
  take seriously.
- **Beat 3 — Colby's return + the decision.** The same old-timer (or the
  gas-station attendant) mentions Colby came back to the valley the last
  few years, lives just past the county line, came back when his knees
  gave out. Jack gets the address; stands outside the place once; does
  not knock — the actual confrontation stays reserved, but the chapter
  ends with the resolve (acted on from Ch. 8): before the festival,
  before the dirt on the parcel gets any deeper, he will go put the
  question to the one man who can say who ordered the ground moved. He
  writes the day's entry — the first real progress entry in the reopened
  file.

- No folder from Emma's perspective in this chapter (that's Ch. 14); no
