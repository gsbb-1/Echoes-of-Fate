# Scene plan: Chapter 10 — "The Notebook"

Two beats, Liam POV: (1) the historical-society archive, where he re-logs
the search-line photograph and runs into Jack; (2) home, digging a box of
his mother's old things for proof his absent father existed, where he finds
the family photograph of a teenage Olivia at the orchard with Daniel at the
edge of frame. Per `scenes/plot-map.md`, neither item "means" anything to
him yet — the significance is the orchard in the background, which he's
just spent weeks staring at for the Harvest Festival exhibit. No posting
(that's Ch. 20); the chapter ends on the almost-connection held in his
notebook.

- **Goal:** make something of the archive work that feels like his own —
  plus, out of nowhere, find something real about the father who phones on
  birthdays half the time.
- **Obstacle:** the digitizing is busywork he was sentenced to; the search
  photo keeps pulling his eye with a composition problem he can't name; and
  the box of his mother's things yields a photograph of a teenage woman in
  the same orchard he's been staring at all week, with a man cut off at the
  frame's edge his mother has never once told him about — only he doesn't
  know enough to read that yet.
- **Turn:** the two images land side by side in his notebook/on his phone —
  archive search line and family photo, same orchard — and instead of
  resolving into a fact they resolve into a hook line for a song he hasn't
  written yet, which is exactly the shape the truth will take when it
  finally gets loose in Ch. 20.
- **Craft note:** Liam's sensory, run-on/impatient voice; music/rhythm
  metaphor ("won't scan," hum-click-clunk), social radar (Mrs. Okafor,
  Jack). The Jack beat is the start of the half-unspoken mentorship
  (`characters/jack.md`) — Jack recognizes the obsession without revealing
  his own reopened case. Physical anchors: cracked phone, spiral notebook
  held together with tape, untuned guitar, uneven undercut.
