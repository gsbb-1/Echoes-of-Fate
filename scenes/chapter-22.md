# Scene plan: Chapter 22 — "Fault Lines"

Olivia POV. Days after Ch. 21. Trust is tested on two fronts — house and
building — and the longest-planned crossing lands: Olivia cleans Emma
Hale's offices two nights a week, and this week the two women finally see
each other. Neither names her secret. The chapter deepens the pressure on
Olivia's silence without spending Ch. 24's confession.

- **Beat 1 — the fault line at home.** Liam comes clean (or the town
  beats him to it): the post, his mother's picture in the society's
  stream, the man at the edge named in the comments. Olivia, blindsided,
  gives her son the smallest truthful version — a friend from the summer
  she was seventeen, gone before the search, nothing she ever needed to
  carry — a sliver that does not drain the climax. Liam struggles to
  reconcile the mother he knows with the girl in the photo. Trust tested,
  not broken.
- **Beat 2 — the crossing.** A cleaning night at the Hale building. The
  offices are still on the remains, and Emma is in late — the two women
  share the empty building's charge. Emma recognizes the diner behind the
  cleaning badge and, startling herself, speaks — a brittle, confessional
  edge about her father running the company into his last years and her
  having "found things out" about him. Olivia's hands go still (the tell;
  never named). Emma catches it and doesn't read it; they part each
  holding the other's near-connection without knowing it. Neither names
  Daniel or the folder. Fault line between the two women, drawn and
  untested.
- **Beat 3 — the crack.** Home: Olivia under the weight of Michael's
  visit (Ch. 21) and Emma's office near-break; her son's question hanging;
  the festival days out. The wall holds — she does NOT go to Jack yet —
  but the reader sees the clock, the love that will soon override, and the
  crack beginning to run.

## Craft notes
- Olivia's ledger/clip interior; the tell (hands, the wipe, the mop
  stroke) doing the dread work. Fragment strictly sensate — nothing
  interpreted.
- The Emma-Olivia scene is a mirror: the woman with the folder and the
  woman with the memory both holding silent. Emma's "found things out"
  stays vague — no folder contents, no explicit naming.
- No confession, no folder on page, no interpretation. ~1,900-2,200
  words.
