# Scene plan: Chapter 19 — "Webs"

Ensemble, anchored on Jack for the confrontation spine. The midpoint
remains-discovery (Ch. 18) is in the air, and the six threads begin to
show their connections — the reader sees the web, the characters only
partially. This chapter also writes the Colby confrontation/admission that
`TASK.md` scheduled for ~Ch. 19: Colby's account gives Jack and Michael
the accident-and-cover-up shape AHEAD of Emma's folder or Olivia's
confession (`scenes/plot-map.md`). Reveal discipline: no folder contents
to anyone (Emma's folder stays at home, unmoved), no Olivia
interpretation, no one asserts "how or by whom" as official fact.

- **Beat 1 — Jack, the diner, the morning after.** The town's one-sentence
  version: human remains, the old orchard, "they think it's that Voss
  boy." Jack reads the room as a web — the strands now visible: the
  parcel, the regrade, the county, Hale's company, the boy who came with
  the postcard, the girl wiping the counter with her back to the room, the
  painter's new piece drawing looks at the café. His decision: before the
  old foreman hears about the discovery and reacts to it, Jack will go
  ask the one man who was there.
- **Beat 2 — Sarah tells Michael (café).** The Hollis ground, finally
  handed over: the orchard and cabin were her family's; they sold the
  ground to Hale Development the very autumn Danny vanished; the cabin
  fragment was a leaving, not a violence. Michael takes it with the whole
  weight — his brother died on ground Sarah's family lost to the Hales the
  same week. The web knots the two threads together for good.
- **Beat 3 — Jack confronts Colby.** Over the county line, Colby's
  retirement place. The old foreman knows why Jack's come. The facts laid
  out (2008 season, the crew, no permit for the north-bank regrade, the
  boy's last week, the severance, the overnight departure) — then the
  crack, and Colby's account in his own voice: Danny found the unfiled
  inspection, threatened to take it to the county, they argued at the pond
  in the dark, the bank gave way, the boy went in and didn't come up;
  panic; the call to Hale; Hale's truck, the machine, "erosion control" in
  the night; the money; leaving town the next week. First time anyone says
  the accident-and-cover-up shape. Colby knows he'll need to say it to the
  county.
- **Beat 4 — Jack lays it for Michael (motel).** Michael sits with the
  shape: accident, not murder; a cover-up, not a killing — driven by the
  man who bought Sarah's family's ground the very week. Michael tells Jack
  the Hollis news; the web completes for the reader (folder and Olivia
  still private). Michael asks: does Hale's daughter know? Jack: "I think
  she knows something. I don't know what." The chapter ends with the two
  of them holding the shape with no official weight under it yet.

## Craft notes
- Ensemble but Jack-anchored; routine POV swaps only at `---` breaks.
  Beats 1 and 4 Jack close-third; Beat 2 Sarah; Beat 3 Jack.
- Colby's voice: old, flat, ashamed, economical — a man who's said it to
  himself in the dark for eighteen years and is almost relieved to say it
  to someone else. The described night matches the on-page fragments the
  reader already holds: the postcard line ("Hale's not going to like"),
  Olivia's headlights/truck (Hale arriving), Emma's folder's check (the
  severance) — all wirings visible to the reader without any of those
  people moving.
- Keep the account untested by any record; Colby's word is the shape, not
  the proof. ~1,900-2,200 words.
