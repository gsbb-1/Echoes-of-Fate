# Scene plan: Chapter 8 — "Echoes of the Unsolved"

Single scene, Jack POV. His house, the small hours, the box on the kitchen
table. Character-Development tier (`scenes/plot-map.md`): Jack moves from
suspicion to partial information — the postcard's line starts to rhyme with
one old note of his own — without anyone else learning anything, and without
Jack reaching a "body" conclusion (that confirmation tier belongs later).

- **Goal:** fold the new postcard into the file and either find the detail
  that fits it or satisfy himself it's nothing.
- **Obstacle:** the file is eighteen years old and thin — the search log
  ends, the three statements say less than they once seemed, no forensics —
  and the one promising penciled note (the north bank of the quarry pond
  regraded "for erosion control" during the search week itself, ordered
  through the foreman, no written work order) had never had a hook to hang
  on.
- **Turn:** the rhyme lands: Daniel worked the parcel Hall Development owned,
  he "found something Hale's not going to like," and the only thing that
  moved on that site in the fortnight around his disappearance was the
  ground by the pond. The document in Daniel's own hand authorizes what
  eighteen years of instinct never could. Jack writes the first entry in his
  own copy of the file in six years — "Reopened." — and knows the cost of
  the next question, and moves it anyway.
- **Craft note:** Cataloguing rhythm; the small-hours detail work as Jack's
  natural habitat; restraint about his own grief (the Carol/cost beat kept
  dry). The ledger echo with Ch. 5 is deliberate — Jack's file is his
  ledger. No reveal of Emma's folder, Olivia's sighting, or Colby's
  departure; the foreman is named only in passing on the regrade note.
