# Scene plan: Chapter 25 — "What's Left Standing"

Ensemble, the day after the reckoning (festival day two). No new reveals —
this chapter is each character's *own* fallout, per
`research/subplot-weaving.md`: the shared secret was the convergence, not
the substance. Weather carried through the town all day; six individual
reckonings, none requiring the mystery anymore.

- **Beat 1 — the town's morning.** Festival day two under the weight of
  what day one told. The diner, the square, the tent: the town carrying
  the corrected story (accident, cover-up) the way it had carried the
  wrong one. People speak Olivia's name differently. Ruth lets it be
  heard.
- **Beat 2 — Jack closes the file.** Jack, at home: the box from the hall
  closet, taken down one last time not to look but to file away. He
  writes the close-out line, seals it, returns the postcard to Michael
  — the inciting object leaving his hands. The ordinary evening (the
  dog, the porch, the diner) finally doesn't need a case under it. His
  reckoning: he can sit at the diner tomorrow for the coffee.
- **Beat 3 — Emma chooses the ground's use.** At Hale Development, with
  the folder's burden gone to the county and the announcement due: the
  board expects spin. Emma instead names her father's part plainly and
  stops the parcel development for good, turning the lakefront to public
  memorial ground — the eighteen-month private stall, at last a chosen
  public act. She takes off her father's watch, and sets it down, and
  keeps it. The reckoning of power and inherited guilt: she doesn't
  repeat him.
- **Beat 4 — Sarah and the painting.** Sarah at the loan corner with
  Diane: her mother, at the festival, standing at last in front of her
  daughter's painting of Hollis ground — the first time Sarah has shown
  her anything that mattered. No apology, just shared silence and the
  one true sentence. Sarah understands she can now paint something
  forward instead of the same ground (the compel is gone; the ground is
  named).
- **Beat 5 — Michael forgives himself.** Michael alone with both
  watches: the broken digital one he's worn since Danny's room, and the
  recovered pocket watch. The last words he said to Daniel, the small
  fight, the guilt he's carried eighteen years — put down on an ordinary
  bench, in daylight, the way Colby put his own down at a porch. He
  calls his mother: "There's something new, Mom. He was found."
- **Beat 6 — Liam and Olivia, true version.** Evening. Mother and son on
  the apartment steps: the sliver (Ch. 22) replaced by the true version,
  said whole and level — who she was, what she saw, why she ran, the
  misread shape, the relief of it. Liam's guilt about the post released
  ("You told the town the truth before I could"). Her self-forgiveness,
  out loud, to him. The post stays up; it is true.
- **Beat 7 — coda.** The festival's last lamp-light; each life standing
  with less weight, in the same town, crossing the same square at
  evening.

## Craft notes
- Ensemble, sectioned, one ordinary day of weather doing for the mood.
- Each reckoning closes a *personal* arc (file, power, family, art,
  self-forgiveness, the mother-son bond) — no new mystery information.
- Michael's beat earns his Ch. 26 close; the pocket watch is a resting
  object, not a new clue. ~2,000-2,250 words.
