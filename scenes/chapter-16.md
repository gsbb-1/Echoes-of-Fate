# Scene plan: Chapter 16 — "The Line Olivia Won't Cross"

Olivia POV. Site work at the parcel advances toward the old pond section;
Olivia is pushed toward the choice she's avoided for eighteen years — and,
this chapter, does NOT cross it. She stays silent; the near-crossing and
its recoil deepen the dread that Ch. 24 will finally resolve (Liam's
danger overrides eighteen years of self-protection — per
`scenes/plot-map.md`). NO remains yet (Ch. 18 owns that): the grading is
scheduled and staged, but the old bank is untouched at chapter's end. No
Olivia interpretation of the fragment — sensory only. No folder contents;
she is nowhere near Emma's credenza.

- **Beat 1 — the diner, midday.** The parcel is on people's lips: crews
  "into the north section by the pond," town talk about what the bulldozers
  will turn up under the old orchard (talk that makes her hands go still —
  the tell shown, never named on-page as hers). Ruth pins up the
  historical society's Harvest Festival anniversary exhibit flyer —
  "Eighteen Years: A Town Remembers" — the archive work (Liam's world)
  made public, on a clock she now knows is short. She serves the room, the
  ledger math running underneath.
- **Beat 2 — the offices, night.** Her Hale Development cleaning shift
  (Ch. 9's job, second-best way of keeping her head where her hands are).
  On a project manager's desk in the shared office — not Emma's locked
  credenza, which she does not touch — a grading schedule and site plan
  shows the north section staged with the pond embankment regrade next up
  ("week of" line). The notch of the old pond on the plan is the shape
  she's kept at her back since seventeen. She reads the date; she puts the
  plan back exactly as it was; the clock has a number now.
- **Beat 3 — the county road at night.** After the shift, the bus home
  corners too early; instead she drives out past the lake (she can borrow
  the neighbor's truck) — stops at the parcel fence in the dark. The
  machinery sits dark and idle beyond the treeline; the ground is quiet.
  The fence is the line. The fragment rises (sensory only): dark water,
  raised voices, a splash, sudden silence, headlights swinging down the
  track and an engine idling, her legs carrying her fast. She almost goes
  under the fence, and doesn't. She drives home with the resolve hardened
  the wrong way: if she goes in now and the dirt gives up what she's
  believed for eighteen years is under it, it ends her and the life
  she's built for the boy. The wall stays up.
- **Beat 4 — home.** Liam is there when she gets in. The near-ask: she
  almost asks him what he's digitizing at the society, almost warns him the
  exhibit is about the man whose search photo he already sees in his sleep;
  she asks about his guitar instead. He talks about the archive photo
  theory he never told the old detective; she lets it sit between them. End
  on the clock and the love for him that will, later, override the fear —
  not yet, but the reader sees the hinge.
