# Scene plan: Chapter 17 — "What Sarah Finds Out"

Sarah POV. The chapter pays the Ch. 12/15 plants and lands Sarah's major
turn (per `scenes/plot-map.md`): she realizes her recurring image is a
real place her own family sold away — and that it is the ground Michael's
brother vanished on. Trigger per plot-map: Michael's reaction to the hung
painting, plus the direct confrontation with her mother. Reveal discipline:
the folder stays with Emma (unread by anyone), Olivia's account stays
unspoken, remains stay unrecovered (that's Ch. 18), no Colby scene yet
(~Ch. 19).

- **Beat 1 — the café, hanging the piece.** Sarah hangs the finished wall
  piece (Ch. 15) before the festival; Owen fusses. Michael comes to see it
  hung, as promised. Alone with the canvas, his controlled stillness on
  the chunk of ground he watched her make gives him away, and she refuses
  the drop-habit (her usual hot-plate avoidance) — she holds, provoking
  the identification. He admits what he's known since the café window
  (Ch. 4): she's been painting where his brother disappeared; he
  recognized it before he knew her name.
- **Beat 2 — the fragment said aloud.** For the first time she says the
  old fragment out loud to anyone (cold water, raised voices she couldn't
  see, being carried, the week her family closed something out when she
  was six). She tells him about the cabin. She resolves, with him, to ask
  what that ground was to her family — the old orchard land (Ch. 12's
  voicemail).
- **Beat 3 — the confrontation with Diane.** Bennett's after close, or the
  house. Sarah asks; Diane deflects and tidies and deflects; Sarah refuses
  to drop it (the arc inversion: her practiced avoiding finally aimed the
  other way). The reveal: the Hollis side owned the orchard and lakeside
  acreage for generations; the cabin was theirs; the same autumn Sarah was
  six they sold the ground to Warren Hale/Hale Development under financial
  hardship and packed the cabin out; Diane married Tom that autumn and has
  never once said the word of it since. Diane knows nothing about the
  mystery — the sale is the loss she's never processed.
- **Beat 4 — the synthesis + resolve.** Sarah refits the fragment against
  the sale week (without over-resolving it — keep the ambiguity per
  plot-map: "very likely" the sale's aftermath). The full realization
  lands: the place she paints is her family's old ground, the place Danny
  Voss's brother has been searching for his entire adult life. She decides
  Michael has to know what his ground was. Ends on the weight she chooses
  to carry rather than drop.

## Craft notes
- Two fat beats (café + mother confrontation); keep Diane's exposition
  tight and painful-in-her-voice (smooth, tidying register; the shop as
  her language). Sarah's growth is structural: where she'd drop it (Ch.
  12/15) she now holds, out loud.
- The identification uses what Ch. 4 established — Michael recognized the
  orchard corner in her work before they met — so it folds back on existing
  canon rather than inventing new reaction.
- Watch ~1,900-2,200 words; the fragment stays sensory and ambiguous.
