# Scene plan: Chapter 12 — "Paths That Shouldn't Cross"

Sarah POV. Coincidence tips into something that feels arranged — each
crossing is real, but the reader (holding Ch. 7's "Michael Voss—Danny's
brother") knows the asymmetry Sarah can't: he's on the ground for his
brother, she's on the ground she paints. The chapter keeps Sarah from
knowing: who he is (first name only), why her place nags her, and any link
to her family's lost cabin — those payloads stay reserved for Ch. 15 and
Ch. 17 respectively.

- **Beat 1 — Saturday market.** Sarah and Michael keep ending up in the
  same frames (market, café line, hardware bench); she's started seeing
  him even when he isn't there. He asks about the piece she's making for
  Owen (Ch. 3's "bring me something whole"); she mentions she's been
  going "out past the lake" to study it. He goes quiet at "lake," a cost
  she can't read yet. First chosen step: they talk without an accident
  happening.
- **Beat 2 — voicemail.** Sarah finally listens to one of her mother's
  stacked voicemails (Ch. 1 established she avoids them). Diane's
  dispatch-of-normal includes "they're finally building out the old
  orchard land." Sarah's flicker toward the cabin and her age-six
  fragment (cold water, raised voices, being carried) — she drops it,
  shelves it, texts her mother something ordinary. The itch is set, not
  resolved; the ch. 17 land-reveal stays unpicked.
- **Beat 3 — the parcel gate (turn).** Sarah is at the fence-line
  sketching when Michael walks up the track. Both startled, then "this
  again," both admitting they've been coming out here. She tells him the
  place is real — that she found the place she's been painting her whole
  working life; he takes it with a weight she misreads as being moved by
  her. He almost tells her why he drove out, pulls back. She offers to
  show him the ground she's painting; he says yes — the relationship
  moves from coincidence to choice, on the unknowing hinge of his
  brother's ground. Reader irony, not Sarah's.
- **Beat 4 — studio button.** Night above the feed store. The canvas she
  sets up for Owen gets the gate-sketch as underpainting — water first,
  not erased this time (her arc move: stop fending the image off). The
  pale line glinting in water she still can't resolve (the watch glint
  plant, Ch. 1) stays a question. She texts her mother back, leaning into
  the arranged feeling instead of away from it.

## Craft notes
- Sarah's associative, tactile, paint-metaphor voice; long clause-stacks
  while absorbed, fragments the instant she's interrupted or performs
  normalcy. Watch the Ch. 4 echo — the interviewer-reads-the-reader
  dynamic, now warming.
- Keep him first-name-only to Sarah; keep her family-sale unknowing;
  keep the chapter's last beat a turn INTO the arrangement, not a reveal.
