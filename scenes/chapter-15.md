# Scene plan: Chapter 15 — "Closer"

Sarah & Michael. The last Sarah-and-Michael chapter before Ch. 17's reveal;
its job is to deepen the relationship and let Michael's real reason for
being in town start to leak through on-page — a leak *felt* by the reader
and partially by Sarah, WITHOUT paying Ch. 17's payload (that the place she
paints is ground her own family sold away, where the search walked). Reveal
discipline per `scenes/plot-map.md`: Sarah may learn WHO he is (the Voss
brother), but must NOT yet connect the parcel she paints to the ground his
brother vanished on; Ch. 17 does that, via Michael's reaction to the
finished painting. No folder, no remains, no Olivia interpretation.

- **Beat 1 — the gate, a few days across.** Michael comes out as promised
  (Ch. 12's ending). Sarah works; he watches; the intimacy of being worked
  beside builds over two or three late-October sessions. The wall piece is
  resolving water-first, bank, treeline, the pale long shine. She notices
  that he watches the place, not her — waiting for it to do something. He
  notices that she paints it like someone remembering, not inventing. The
  exchange about "standing watch" turns real: she asks him directly what
  he's looking for.
- **Beat 2 — the leak.** He tells her, in parts, the true thing: his
  brother was twenty-two and came to Willow Creek for a summer crew job
  and disappeared a week into autumn, eighteen years, never closed for
  proof, and Michael is here to find the place and the men who knew what
  happened. He names him — Danny — and lets his own surname (Voss) hang in
  the air. Sarah hears it the way the town hears it, the case-name reflex,
sharpened now: the historical society's anniversary exhibit (Liam's
project, the work that keeps getting talked about at the café) is
resurfacing the same
  story. She fits the stranger to the town's ghost. She asks why he's told
  her; he tells her the truth of it plain — once the town knows the
  brother's back, everyone starts performing, and he's spent a life
  cataloguing people who perform. She keeps the secret; it becomes their
  secret, which is the "closer."
- **Beat 3 — coda, Michael POV (short).** Walking the dark county road
  alone afterward. He has finally seen the finished piece whole and
  recognized the ground — treeline, the old bank, the north edge — as the
  place he's spent eighteen years reconstructing from a postcard. He did
  not tell her. He notes the pale glint in the paint, the corner that keeps
  sliding away from him, and decides he'll know it later for what it is.
  The reader holds the irony: she paints his brother's ground and neither
  of them can say it yet. Ends on his resolve to see her reveal the rest
  of the place to him when she's ready.

## Craft notes
- Architecture: Sarah POV for Beats 1-2 (her interior is the warmth of the
  chapter), a hard `---` break, then the short Michael coda — honors the
  outline's "Sarah & Michael" dual-POV designation without roving.
- Sarah: associative paint metaphors, fragments under interruption, the
  performed-normalcy lightness when a thing churns under it. Michael:
  exact, careful, dry; sentences he tests and doesn't send. Keep his
  catalogue-man irony intact (Ch. 4/7).
- Watch the word budget (~1,900-2,200). The leak should land through
  dialogue and her reading of him, not exposition.
- Keep the postcard text OFF this chapter — it's Ch. 17+ material and would
  spend the reveal early. Sarah learns who, not what.
