# Scene plan: Chapter 20 — "Liam's Reckoning"

Liam POV. The connective moment per `scenes/plot-map.md`: not grasping the
weight of what he has, Liam posts the archive search-line photo beside the
family photo of his teenage mother at the orchard (Ch. 10 established both,
and that he knows the girl is his mother, and that he stopped himself from
posting once). Chapter 20 is the post going live and the first ripple that
teaches him what he's done; Ch. 21 opens with it reaching Michael.

- **Beat 1 — the itch reignites.** Home/breakfast; the remains news has
  made the old case the town's live story. At the archive, the exhibit
  prep is suddenly charged; Mrs. Okafor; the working folder; the notebook
  line ("she's standing in both, still"). He pulls the two photos up on
  his phone again, summer against search.
- **Beat 2 — the post.** Wanting one real thing he found to count (his
  father who phones on birthdays; a boy nobody takes seriously), he uses
  the society's exhibit account (he has the login as the digitizing
  volunteer) to post the two photos side by side — his mother in the
  orchard the summer before the search, captioned as an interesting
  coincidence, "she never mentioned it." He hesitates (the Ch. 10
  instinct) and posts anyway; the remains news is what tips him.
- **Beat 3 — the reckoning.** Within hours, comments; a share; Mrs.
  Okafor's look. Then a comment that stops him: the young man in the work
  shirt at the edge of the summer photo looks like the young man in the
  Voss coverage. He pulls the exhibit folder's photo of Daniel Voss and
  compares — the click: the edge of his mother's kept photograph is the
  missing man, and his mother was standing beside him in the orchard, the
  summer before, and she never once said a word about any of it. He
  understands, too late, what he has let out — his mother at the center of
  the town's headline, the same week they found the body. He goes to take
  it down and can't bring himself to / it's already turned; he lets it
  stand. End: the post living in the dark, his mother asleep in the next
  room, the web closing.

## Craft notes
- Liam's voice: sensory/impatient, run-ons, half-song notebooks, untuned
  guitar; the itch he can't name; wanting to be taken seriously.
- Continuity: the family photo is *the orchard, before* (summer, in
  leaf); the search photo is the bare rows; the girl is ~17 (Ch. 10's
  prose). He knows the girl is his mother — do not use plot-map's
  "doesn't recognize" (superseded by Ch. 10's draft).
- No Olivia POV/reveal here; her story stays hers. The post persists so
  Ch. 21's "reaches Michael" holds. ~1,900-2,200 words.
