# Pacing and chapter length for a 26-chapter / ~200-page novel

Guidance for distributing story across the expanded structure, and how it
maps onto the existing 6-part thematic skeleton in `manuscript/outline.md`.

## Target chapter length

~200 pages across 26 chapters averages **roughly 7-8 pages per chapter**
(around 1,800-2,200 words at standard manuscript formatting, ~250-300
words/page). That's a normal contemporary-fiction chapter length — long
enough for one full scene plus a short second beat, short enough to keep
rotating-POV momentum. Chapters don't need to be equal length: a tense,
single-scene chapter can run 3-4 pages; a chapter carrying two scenes for
one character can run 10-12. What matters is that each chapter earns its
own length rather than defaulting to a fixed word count (the failure mode
of the current ~470-word Chapter 1, which is a summary, not a scene).

## Scenes per chapter

Most chapters should contain **one primary scene**, occasionally two if the
second is short and functions as a button/turn on the first (e.g., a scene
ends, then a short coda shows the consequence landing). Avoid cramming
multiple full scenes for multiple characters into one chapter — that's the
current Chapter 1 problem at chapter scale (six characters, no scene, all
summary) and would recur even in a longer chapter if six POVs were forced
into one.

## Distributing act structure across 26 chapters

A useful rough allocation (traditional four-part shape mapped onto 26
chapters):

- **Setup (~chapters 1-6, ~23%):** establish each POV character in a real
  scene with a want/obstacle (not an introduction montage), plant the
  connective mechanism between the six threads, get Sarah and Michael's
  first meeting on the page early (currently slated for ch. 2).
- **Rising action, part 1 (~chapters 7-13, ~27%):** individual threads
  develop in parallel — Emma's secret under pressure, Jack's case
  reopening, Olivia/Liam conflict escalating, Sarah/Michael relationship
  deepening. Threads still mostly separate but starting to touch.
- **Rising action, part 2 / midpoint turn (~chapters 14-19, ~23%):**
  threads visibly intersect; secrets start to leak; stakes escalate faster.
  A strong midpoint reversal around chapter 13-14 (halfway) gives the back
  half urgency.
- **Climax (~chapters 20-23, ~15%):** the six characters converge, per the
  outline's existing climax beat — this likely needs more than one chapter
  now that there's room, since a six-character convergence has multiple
  characters' worth of confrontation and revelation to stage in scene
  rather than summary.
- **Resolution/denouement (~chapters 24-26, ~12%):** individual
  reckonings and the "twist" revealing how the six stories were connected
  all along, then a short closing reflection chapter.

## Mapping onto the current 6-part thematic structure

The existing 6-part skeleton (Introduction / Character development / Plot /
Climax / Resolution / Conclusion) still works as the thematic spine; the
26-chapter breakdown is a finer-grained scene-by-scene realization of it,
not a replacement. Rough correspondence:

| 6-part beat | Approx. chapters (of 26) |
|---|---|
| Introduction | 1-4 |
| Character development | 5-9 |
| Plot (threads intertwine) | 10-17 |
| Climax | 18-22 |
| Resolution | 23-25 |
| Conclusion | 26 |

This roughly doubles the old 13-chapter breakdown's beats (each old chapter
becomes ~2 new chapters), which fits the "split into setup + consequence"
approach suggested in `multi-pov-structure.md`.

## Practical pacing checks while drafting

- **No chapter should be pure setup with nothing at stake in-scene** — even
  early "introduction" chapters need a want/obstacle playing out in real
  time (see `show-dont-tell.md`).
- **Vary chapter length with tension, not habit.** Short, fast chapters
  work well right before or after a big reveal; longer chapters suit slower
  relationship or investigation scenes.
- **Watch POV gaps.** No POV character should go dark for more than ~4-5
  chapters in the middle third, or their thread loses momentum for the
  reader (see the "last seen" ledger suggestion in `multi-pov-structure.md`).
- **End chapters on a turn, not a summary.** A chapter should end where
  something has just changed or is about to, not where the narration has
  wrapped everything up neatly — the opposite of how the current Chapter 1
  closes.
