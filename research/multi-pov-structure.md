# Multi-POV ensemble structure

Notes on organizing chapter-to-POV assignment across six characters and
~26 chapters, for expanding past the current 13-chapter outline.

## How many POV characters is manageable

Six rotating close-third POV characters is on the high end of typical for a
single-volume novel but well within range for the genre this project sits
in (multi-strand contemporary drama/mystery). The practical risk at six
isn't "too many" in principle — it's that each character gets too few
chapters to build a distinct arc the reader can track, especially early.
Two things keep six POVs readable:

- **Uneven allocation is normal and expected.** Ensemble novels rarely give
  every POV character exactly the same number of chapters. A common pattern
  is 2-3 "anchor" POVs that carry more real estate (here: plausibly Sarah
  and Michael, since they're the romance/mystery throughline) with the
  other four rotating through fewer, more targeted chapters.
- **Pairing POVs by relationship, not just by rotation order.** Because this
  cast already has natural pairs (Sarah/Michael as the central relationship,
  Olivia/Liam as mother/son, Emma/Jack as linked through the case), chapters
  work best when a POV character's chapter is triggered by a plot beat
  involving their paired character, not by mechanical round-robin.

## Chapter-to-POV assignment patterns

Three common approaches, in order of how much they suit this project:

1. **Beat-driven rotation (recommended here):** the POV for a chapter is
   whichever character is at the center of the next plot beat, not a fixed
   rotation. This avoids the "checking in on everyone" feeling and lets
   momentum build — e.g., two Sarah/Michael chapters in a row during their
   courtship, then a Jack/Emma chapter when the case beat needs to move.
2. **Fixed round-robin:** each chapter cycles to the next character in a set
   order. Easiest to plan, but risks feeling mechanical, and forces scenes
   to exist for characters even when nothing new is happening to them —
   likely to reproduce the current Chapter 1 problem (six characters getting
   equal, shallow treatment) if used strictly.
3. **Clustered arcs:** the book is divided into sections, each centered on
   one or two characters' arcs with others in periphery, then a final
   section braids all six together for the climax. This maps well onto the
   existing 6-part thematic skeleton in `manuscript/outline.md` and could
   be layered under a beat-driven rotation at the chapter level.

## Keeping six threads from feeling disconnected across 26 chapters

- **Establish the connective mechanism early and concretely.** Per
  `PLAN.md`'s open structural gap #2, the six fates need a specific shared
  hook (event, location, secret) — without it, rotating POV reads as six
  parallel short stories rather than one novel. This is a story-design
  decision, not a prose technique, but it's the single biggest lever for
  ensemble cohesion.
- **Let POV characters appear in each other's chapters before they get
  their own POV chapter together.** Seeing Michael from Sarah's POV in
  Chapter 1 or 2, before Michael gets his own extended POV chapter, builds
  anticipation and makes the cast feel like one town rather than six
  separate books.
- **Cross-reference concrete details, not just theme.** A place, object, or
  overheard line mentioned in one character's chapter should resurface,
  recontextualized, in another's. This is more binding than the shared
  abstract theme ("fate/choice") the outline currently relies on.
- **Use chapter-end hooks that point at another character's thread**, so
  the reader's momentum carries across POV switches instead of resetting.
- **Track a "last seen" ledger per character** (which chapter, what changed
  for them) so no POV character disappears for so many chapters that the
  reader forgets their stakes — a common failure mode past ~20 chapters.
  A simple table in `manuscript/outline.md` or a new `scenes/` tracking
  file would serve this.

## Applying to the 13 -> 26 chapter expansion

Each existing 13-chapter beat can likely split into two: one chapter that
dramatizes the setup/decision in scene for the lead POV of that beat, and a
second that shows the consequence or a paired character's reaction. That
preserves the current plot shape while giving every beat room to be a real
scene instead of a summary — see `pacing-and-chapter-length.md` for how
that maps across 26 chapters.
